# KK Reel Formats — Instagram

> Library of Instagram **Reel** format templates. Companion to `kk-post.md` (LinkedIn text) and
> `kk-carousel.md` (LinkedIn carousels). The [bracketed stage directions] are the FORMAT — never
> change them. The lines under each direction are the worked EXAMPLES, to be filled in the
> owner's voice. Until then they read `[xxx]`.
>
> **A reel is not a post read aloud.** A post is read; a reel is watched. The caption does not
> carry the hook — the first spoken sentence does. Everything below follows from that.
>
> **When filling the brackets, apply the "HUMAN TEXTURE" section of `.claude/rules/anti-ai.md`:**
> open on a moment of recognition, start sentences with "And"/"But" where it fits, use casual
> markers in moderation, write to one real person, keep it concrete. Stay inside the bans. The
> format gives structure; the texture makes it sound like the owner, not an AI.

## Where these came from

Built 2026-08-18 from a study of **@bryanlowwww** (863K followers) — live API engagement on 12
posts, full uncut captions, and four video transcripts supplied by the owner. Research:
`AI Control Centre/reference/findings/07-bryan-low-instagram.md`.

**Four of his formats were studied. Two are unusable here** and are documented in the
"Formats deliberately excluded" section at the end, with the reason, so nobody rebuilds them.

## The register

`references/voice-profile.md → Platform register` already sets it:

> **LinkedIn (primary):** colleague conversation. Professional, informed, structured,
> recommendation-driven.
>
> **Instagram: coffee conversation. Looser, more personal, raw — journal reflections, inner
> processing.**

LinkedIn's "here's my recommendation based on the analysis" is **wrong** on a reel. Thinking out
loud is correct here, not sloppy.

## Reel principles (govern every template)

1. **The first spoken sentence is the hook.** Not the caption. A viewer decides in about two
   seconds, and they are listening, not reading.
2. **Do NOT score reel hooks with `hook-checklist.md`.** That file scores a LinkedIn text post's
   opening line, where the line is all there is. A reel has a face, a voice, a setting and two
   minutes. Judge reels on views, watch-through and DMs — real numbers, not a rubric.
   *(Evidence: a reel whose caption scores 2/5 on that checklist pulled 85,400 views.)*
3. **Length 60–180 seconds.** Measured from his four most recent: 54s, 133s, 150s, 179s.
   Longer than reflex says. Room to actually think.
4. **Talk over an ordinary activity.** Hands busy, eyes off the lens, natural pauses. It removes
   stiffness without acting. It also SHOWS the life instead of claiming it, which is the only
   version compliance allows.
5. **One take.** If it needs a second take the topic wasn't ready. Move on, come back.
6. **Never announce credentials.** `voice-profile.md` HARD NOS. The reference account opens with
   "I've grown over 1.5 million followers" — that exact move is banned here. Every template below
   opens on a **situation** instead. See "The opening problem" below.
7. **Decide the last line before filming.** Close on a specific insight, one clear action, or one
   unanswered question. Never a motivational-poster line, never a trailing "…".
8. **Caption ≠ hook.** Reach reels: caption under 40 characters, no ask, withholds. Lead reels:
   long caption, specific promise, "DM me". Nothing in between.
9. **Lived and ChatGPT-proof.** If the lines could come from ChatGPT they fail the litmus test.
   Never invent — leave `[xxx — needs your real X]`.
10. **No overlap, ever.** Each reel stands on its own story and angle. Themes recur;
    illustrations don't.
11. **Compliance.** Examples are CAREER/COACHING (Mode 1). Anything financial flips to Mode 2 —
    concept only, no numbers, no products, no returns, and the contrarian edge is capped. See
    `references/iafa-compliance.md`.
12. **Run qa-gate before shipping.** Voice, anti-AI, compliance, cadence.

## The opening problem, and how these templates solve it

On LinkedIn the headline and photo answer *"why should I listen to you?"* before you speak. On
Instagram a scrolling stranger has no idea who you are.

The reference account fills that gap with a credential dump: *"I've grown over 1.5 million
followers across platforms, been invited to speak on stages, work with brands around the
world."* **That is a HARD NO here.**

**The replacement is a situation, not a credential.** Evidence it works — from
`references/learning/hook-performance.md`, 86 rows, 59 aged-in, quartiles computed 2026-08-16:

| Top-quartile hook | Score | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "For the past year I've worked 8am to 10pm" | **132** | confession |
| "Some nights remind you what matters most" | **125** | — |
| "Stepping back was the hardest thing I ever did" | **82** | confession |
| "I didn't burn out because I couldn't handle it" | **49** | confession |
| "Being the one who fixes everything isn't leadership" | **47** | reframe |

**Four of the top six are confessions.** No credentials in any of them. Bottom quartile is
declarative statements about money and status (scores 10–12).

The reference account's own best post is also a self-examination question — *"what if you're
actually the toxic one?"*, 20,193 likes. **Two unrelated data sets agree.** Lead with the
question or the confession, never the credential.

## The three opening moves (use one, never a credential)

**O1. The confession.** A cost you paid, stated flat. *"For the past year I've worked 8am to
10pm."* Highest-scoring pattern in the ledger.

**O2. The self-examination question.** Turn the mirror on the viewer. *"What if you're the
bottleneck?"* The reference account's top post is this move.

**O3. The situation.** A scene with a time and a place. *"It's 10pm and I'm still at my desk."*
Concrete before abstract, per the voice profile.

## The mix

| Type | Share | Families | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | 3 of 4 | A (inversion), C (story) | **None** |
| **Lead** | 1 of 4 | B (teaching) | **"DM me"** |

Inversion and story reels end on a question or an insight; bolting a CTA on breaks them.
Teaching reels have just given something, so an ask is proportionate.

**Four a week, one batch.** The reference account runs 4–5 a week, but half his output is
sponsored or borrowed. Every reel here has to be the owner's own, which caps the cadence.

## Formats deliberately excluded

Documented so nobody rebuilds them.

**The sponsored reel.** The reference account's highest comment ratios come from affiliate posts
for an AI video tool, with a keyword gate ("comment SAHARA / Wolves / SEEDANCE / hustle"). Four
of his five lead posts are this. **Excluded:** a keyword lead-gen gate is bucket B (15 working
days) on anything financial, product content in her own words is bucket C, and the reel that
carries it also says "investing means you already need money to make money" and "a money making
machine" — income framing, bucket C. It is also his worst-performing format at 920 likes against
20,193 for a psychology hook.

**The motivational compilation reel.** Twelve borrowed one-liners read over video ("your
bloodline waited for centuries", "some people are never gonna clap for you", "God put that dream
in your heart"). **Excluded on five counts:** `voice-profile.md` — "if ChatGPT could've produced
it, it's worthless and not hers"; `anti-ai.md` — motivational-poster endings banned; "never make
claims without verifiable proof"; IAFA slide 23 — plagiarised or uncited content is bucket C;
IAFA slide 23 — **religious content is bucket C**.

**The credential-dump opening.** Not a format but a move, used in his teaching reels: "I've
grown over 1.5 million followers, been invited to speak on stages, work with brands around the
world." **Excluded:** `voice-profile.md` HARD NOS — "never announce credentials or self-promote".
Replaced by the three opening moves above, which the ledger shows perform better anyway.
