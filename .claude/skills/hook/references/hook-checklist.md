# Textual Hook Effectiveness Checklist

> A scoring reference for anyone writing social media hooks — editors, clients, or Claude roles.
> Think of hooks like headlines. They must grab attention and make people *feel* something fast.
> Use this checklist to diagnose why a hook lands or falls flat.

---

## How to Use This Document

Every hook should be scored against the 5 elements below. Each element is worth 1 point. A hook needs **4/5 or higher** to ship. Below 3 — rewrite from scratch.

Read the element, check the hook against it, then look at the industry examples to calibrate your judgment.

---

## The 5 Elements

### Element 1: Short and Punchy

**The rule:** 2 lines maximum. Every word earns its place. If you can cut a word and the hook still works, cut it.

**What to check:**
- Is it 2 lines or fewer?
- Can you read it in under 3 seconds?
- Are there any filler words ("just", "really", "actually", "that", "very") you can remove without losing meaning?
- Does it hit hard on first read — or does it require a second pass to understand?

**Calibration — Education (Tuition):**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "As a parent, have you ever wondered if the tuition centre your child is attending is actually helping them improve their grades and build confidence?" | Way too long. Buried in qualifiers. | "Your child's tuition bill went up. Their grades didn't." | 10 words. Contrast creates instant tension. |
| "We believe that every student deserves a personalized approach to learning that helps them reach their full potential" | Corporate mission statement, not a hook. | "3 hours of tuition a week. Zero improvement. Sound familiar?" | Specific. Painful. Ends with a question that forces a nod. |

**Calibration — Insurance Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Many people don't realize the importance of having adequate insurance coverage until something unexpected happens to them or their family" | Reads like a textbook. Zero urgency. | "Your hospital bill won't wait for your next paycheck." | One line. Real scenario. Immediate gut reaction. |
| "Did you know that critical illness claims have been increasing year over year and most people are underinsured?" | Stat-dump opening. No emotion. | "Nobody plans to get cancer at 35. But somebody reading this will." | Uncomfortable truth. Personal. Impossible to scroll past. |

**Calibration — Real Estate Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "If you're thinking about buying your first home in Singapore, there are many important factors you need to consider before making this big financial decision" | Sounds like a government pamphlet. | "You saved for 5 years. You still can't afford the flat you wanted." | Specific pain. Emotionally loaded timeline. |
| "The property market is showing interesting trends that homebuyers and investors should be paying attention to right now" | Vague. No stakes. No feeling. | "Your colleague just made $80K on a condo flip. You're still renting." | Comparison triggers envy and urgency — both high-emotion. |

### Element 2: Promises a Gain or Threatens a Pain

**The rule:** Every hook sits on one of two foundations — "here's what you could win" or "here's what you're losing." Neutral hooks are invisible hooks. People scroll past information. They stop for stakes.

**What to check:**
- Can you clearly identify the gain or pain in the hook?
- Is the gain/pain *specific* (not vague like "improve your life" or "avoid mistakes")?
- Would the reader feel something — desire, fear, regret, relief — within the first second?
- Does it answer the reader's instinctive question: "Why should I care?"

**Calibration — Education (Tuition):**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "5 tips to help your child study better" | Generic gain. No emotional weight. | "Your child isn't lazy. They just haven't been taught how to learn." | Reframes the pain. Defends the child. Parent feels seen AND relieved. |
| "Learning shouldn't be stressful for students" | True but flat. No stakes. | "Every year your child falls behind in Math, the gap gets harder to close." | Escalating pain. Time pressure. The cost of inaction is clear. |

**Calibration — Insurance Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Protect your family's future with comprehensive coverage" | So generic it could be a banner ad from 2005. | "If you died tomorrow, how many months could your family survive on savings alone?" | Gain framed through pain. Forces mental calculation. Impossible to ignore. |
| "Why insurance is important for young professionals" | Title of an article nobody clicks. | "You're 28, healthy, and think insurance is a waste. That's exactly what my last claimant thought too." | Pain through someone else's story. The "too" implies consequence. |

**Calibration — Real Estate Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Now is a great time to invest in property" | Every agent says this. Zero specificity. | "The flat you liked in 2022 is now $120K more. The one you like today won't wait either." | Specific regret (past) + urgency (present). Double pain. |
| "Understanding the en-bloc process and what it means for homeowners" | Educational but no hook energy. | "Your neighbour got $1.2M from the en-bloc. You voted no. Let's talk about what that cost you." | Pain of a real missed opportunity. Hyper-specific. |

### Element 3: Open Loop or Curiosity Marker

**The rule:** The hook should create an itch that only the rest of the post can scratch. An open loop is an unanswered question, incomplete story, or teased insight that pulls the reader forward. If your hook resolves itself — there's no reason to keep reading.

**What to check:**
- After reading the hook, does the reader *need* to know what comes next?
- Is there a question (stated or implied) that hasn't been answered yet?
- Does the hook tease a "what", "how", or "why" without giving it away?
- Would someone tap "see more" or swipe to slide 2?

**Calibration — Education (Tuition):**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Here are 3 study techniques that work for primary school students." | Closed loop. I already know what I'm getting. No pull. | "My student went from failing Math to top 10 in class. But it wasn't the tuition that fixed it." | Wait — if it wasn't the tuition, what was it? Open loop forces the click. |
| "Good study habits start young." | Statement of fact. No curiosity. | "The highest-scoring students in my class all have one thing in common. It's not talent." | "What is it?" — the reader can't help asking. |

**Calibration — Insurance Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "3 reasons why you need critical illness coverage." | Closed. I can already guess all three. | "My client had $200K in savings. After 6 months in hospital, she had $11K left. Here's what nobody told her." | Story + mystery. "What nobody told her" is the open loop. |
| "Don't wait until it's too late to get insured." | Cliche. No curiosity gap. | "There's one question I ask every new client. Most of them go silent." | What's the question? Why do they go silent? Double curiosity. |

**Calibration — Real Estate Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "5 things first-time buyers need to know." | Listicle energy. No pull. | "I told my client not to buy the condo everyone wanted. 6 months later, she thanked me." | Contrarian advice + positive outcome = what happened in between? |
| "The property market is changing — here's what you need to know." | Vague. Every agent posts this. | "There's a clause in your HDB lease most owners have never read. It could cost you $50K." | Specific hidden risk. "What clause?" is irresistible. |

### Element 4: Identity or Audience Callout

**The rule:** When people see themselves in a hook — their role, their group, their situation, their values — they feel it *instantly*. Identity is one of the strongest emotional triggers. Calling out a specific group makes the hook feel like it was written for them, not for everyone (which means no one).

**What to check:**
- Can the reader immediately say "that's me" or "that's my people"?
- Is a specific group, role, life stage, or identity named or strongly implied?
- Does the hook *exclude* people who aren't in the target group? (Good hooks repel the wrong audience.)
- Would this feel personal if shared in a group chat of the target audience?

**Calibration — Education (Tuition):**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Every student can improve with the right support." | Speaks to everyone = speaks to no one. | "Kiasu parents, this one's for you. You signed your kid up for 4 enrichment classes. Ask yourself — are they learning, or just busy?" | "Kiasu parents" is an identity callout in Singapore. Immediately personal. The question stings because it's true. |
| "Struggling students need more help." | Generic. No identity hook. | "Your P6 child is studying 4 hours a night and still not hitting AL1. Something's broken — and it's not their effort." | "P6 child" + "AL1" = hyper-specific to Singapore PSLE parents. They feel *seen*. |

**Calibration — Insurance Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Everyone needs a financial safety net." | No identity. Feels like a PSA. | "Millennials in Singapore: you have a condo, a car loan, and $0 in critical illness coverage. We need to talk." | Age group + country + lifestyle + gap = "that's literally me." |
| "Insurance is important for young families." | Weak callout. "Young families" is too broad. | "You just had your first baby. Your MediShield Life covers $0 of NICU. How does that feel?" | New parents + specific coverage gap. The question forces an emotional response. |

**Calibration — Real Estate Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Thinking of upgrading your home?" | Could be anyone. No pull. | "BTO owners who hit MOP this year — the decision you make in the next 6 months could be worth $200K." | "BTO owners who hit MOP" is a razor-sharp identity slice. Deadline creates urgency. |
| "Property investment tips for beginners." | Generic. No emotional entry point. | "You're a tech PM earning $8K/month. You're 32. You still think property is 'not for you yet.' Let me show you the math." | Role + salary + age + belief = the reader can't look away because it's describing their exact situation. |

### Element 5: Contention, Contrarian, or Controversial

**The rule:** Hooks that challenge a widely held belief, poke a sacred cow, or take an unpopular stance create friction — and friction stops the scroll. People engage with what they disagree with just as strongly as what they agree with. The goal isn't to be offensive. It's to say the thing everyone's thinking but nobody's posting.

**What to check:**
- Does the hook challenge a default assumption or common advice?
- Would at least 30% of readers instinctively disagree? (If everyone agrees, it's not contrarian — it's a platitude.)
- Is the tension *earned* — rooted in real experience or logic — or is it just edgy for clicks?
- Would this spark comments? Would people tag someone or say "finally someone said it"?

**Calibration — Education (Tuition):**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Tuition is important for academic success." | Agreeable. Safe. Invisible. | "Tuition is making your child worse at learning. Here's why." | Directly attacks something the reader is *paying for*. Contrarian + open loop. |
| "Every child learns differently." | True and boring. | "The best student in my class? Zero tuition. The worst? Three tutors. Nobody wants to talk about why." | Challenges the assumption that more tuition = better results. The "nobody wants to talk about why" adds controversy. |

**Calibration — Insurance Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "It's never too early to plan for retirement." | Safe advice. No edge. | "Your insurance agent doesn't want you to read your own policy. Here's what they're hoping you skip." | Takes a shot at the industry. Contrarian because agents are supposed to be trusted. Curiosity built in. |
| "Having the right coverage gives peace of mind." | Platitude. | "Whole life insurance is the most overpaid product in Singapore. And most of you own it." | Direct challenge to a popular product + "most of you own it" makes it personal. Guaranteed debate in comments. |

**Calibration — Real Estate Agents:**

| Bad | Why it misses | Good | Why it hits |
|-----|--------------|------|-------------|
| "Location is the most important factor in property." | Everyone knows this. Not a hook. | "Stop buying property 'near MRT.' That advice made sense in 2015. It's costing you money in 2026." | Attacks conventional wisdom with a time-specific reframe. Contrarian + specific. |
| "Now is a good time to invest." | Every agent says this in every market. | "I'm a property agent and I'm telling you — don't buy a condo right now. Here's why." | Agent advising *against* buying? That's contrarian to their own financial interest. Instant credibility + curiosity. |

## Quick-Score Card

Use this when reviewing any hook. Score 1 point per element present:

| # | Element | Check | Score (0 or 1) |
|---|---------|-------|----------------|
| 1 | **Short & Punchy** | 2 lines max, no filler, hits in under 3 seconds | |
| 2 | **Gain or Pain** | Clear, specific promise or threat the reader *feels* | |
| 3 | **Open Loop / Curiosity** | Creates an itch only the rest of the post can scratch | |
| 4 | **Identity Callout** | Reader instantly says "that's me" or "that's my people" | |
| 5 | **Contention / Contrarian** | Challenges a default belief; at least 30% would disagree | |

**Scoring:**

- **5/5** — Ship it. This hook earns the scroll-stop.
- **4/5** — Strong. Check which element is missing — can you layer it in without adding length?
- **3/5** — Mediocre. Likely gets scrolled past. Rewrite with the missing elements in mind.
- **2/5 or below** — Start over. The hook isn't doing its job.

## Combining Elements — What Great Hooks Look Like

The best hooks stack 4-5 elements together. Here's how they layer:

**Education:** "Kiasu parents — your child has 3 tutors and still isn't improving. The problem isn't effort. It's something no tuition centre will tell you."
- Short and punchy (2 lines)
- Pain (child not improving despite investment)
- Open loop ("something no tuition centre will tell you")
- Identity ("Kiasu parents")
- Contrarian (implies tuition centres are hiding something)

**Insurance:** "You're 30, Singaporean, and you have $0 in critical illness coverage. Your MediShield won't save you. And your agent probably never explained why."
- Short and punchy (2 lines)
- Pain (exposed financial gap)
- Open loop ("never explained why")
- Identity ("30, Singaporean")
- Contrarian (challenges trust in agents and government healthcare)

**Real Estate:** "Every property agent will tell you to buy near an MRT. I'm telling you that advice just lost my client $60K. Here's what actually matters."
- Short and punchy (2 lines)
- Pain ($60K loss)
- Open loop ("here's what actually matters")
- Identity (implied — people following conventional property advice)
- Contrarian (agent going against industry's most repeated advice)

## Diagnostic: Why Hooks Fail

When a hook scores below 4, it usually fails for one of these reasons:

**Too long.** The writer tried to explain instead of hook. A hook's only job is to earn the next line. Cut everything that explains.

**Too safe.** The writer avoided anything that could be disagreed with. If nobody would argue with your hook, nobody will stop for it either.

**Too vague.** "Improve your life" and "avoid common mistakes" say nothing. Specificity is what makes hooks feel real. A dollar amount, a timeline, a role, a scenario — these are what create the feeling.

**No open loop.** The hook resolves itself. "Here are 5 tips for better sleep" — I already know what I'm getting. There's no pull. Compare: "I fixed my sleep in 3 days. But the solution had nothing to do with sleep."

**No identity.** The hook speaks to "everyone" which means it speaks to no one. The moment you name a group — BTO owners, P6 parents, tech PMs, millennials — the right people lean in and the wrong people self-select out. Both are good.
